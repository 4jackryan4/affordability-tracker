How these numbers are calculated

  • Baseline. Every change is measured from January 2025. For monthly series that is the January 2025 reading. For weekly series (gas, diesel, mortgage rates) it is the average of every week in January 2025, so one unusual week can't skew it.
  • Latest. The most recent reading the agency has published. Monthly BLS data runs about six weeks behind; weekly data runs a few days behind.
  • Percent change is (latest − baseline) ÷ baseline. If rounding the prices shown would give a different percent, the page shows an extra decimal place so anyone re-checking the math gets the same answer.
  • Seasonal series. Electricity and utility gas prices are not seasonally adjusted and swing with the weather. For those, the year-over-year change is the cleaner comparison and is shown on each card.
  • Since the Iran war began. On gas, diesel, mortgage rates and overall prices, a second line shows the change from the last reading before the war began on Feb. 28, 2026 (the last week of February for weekly data; February for monthly data) to the latest reading. It appears only where higher fuel and oil costs from the war are a documented driver. It measures the change since that date; other factors can contribute.
  • While tariffs were in effect. On coffee and bananas, a line shows the change from March 2025, the last reading before tariffs took effect on April 5, 2025, to November 2025, when both were exempted from tariffs on Nov. 13. Economists cite tariffs as one contributor to these increases, alongside weather and supply. The shaded band on those charts marks the tariff period.
  • Mortgage payments assume a new 30-year fixed loan of $400,000, principal and interest only.
  • Where the data comes from. All series are pulled through FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis), which republishes the agencies' official figures unchanged. Each card links to both the agency and the FRED series.
